Barnaby · the film · v2

Script gate 1 · for your notes

A product film about a family, a desk and the old system they run on, with Barnaby arriving as the newest member of the household. Around 2:55. Everything here is open to your notes before anything is built.

The world bible behind it: video/v2/WORLD.md (people, the desk and its props, how Barnaby speaks, era rules). The look book (gate 2) and an animatic (gate 3) come before any full render.

What changed from v1

Your noteWhat v2 does
Captions told the story; UI on a dark gridPeople and a place tell it. Every shot is the desk diorama or the screen inside it. Only the closing titles use on-screen text. One quiet "engine note" line carries the Synapse facts for judges.
Barnaby talks like a developer ("ordering endpoint")He speaks Martin's language, asks before taking a key, explains before changing anything. Same rule in the real app.
Martin can't judge a code diffBarnaby explains the change: what it does, what it reads, that it changes nothing, that Theo's checks pass. The code is behind "see the change".
"Twenty-four things. Theo wrote twenty-three…" is textbook AILines are written for characters with histories: Theo's jokes, Martin's "Go on, then", Priya's dry "Noted.", Barnaby's restraint.
Personality like "Reports (coming soon!!)"It becomes the spine of the Build beat: the room Theo never built, finally finished. Props and sticky notes carry more of it.
Quorum and ComfyUI were just cardsTheir own beat: Theo's new job, a brand-new house picked up in seconds. Staged and labelled as such.
Speech too slowEach character gets a measured pace from real speaking-rate research (the targets below are provisional until SPEECH.md lands).

The device that makes Synapse legible

Synapse gives Barnaby the floor plan of the house. Martin gives him the keys. On Barnaby's screen, Theo's system appears as a floor plan: each room is something the system can do (Jobs, Customers, Parts, Orders, Reports…). Lit rooms are ones Barnaby can use. A key icon means he could ask for it. Compose draws a service passage between rooms he keeps walking. Build adds a room. A one-line engine note in the corner gives the technical truth for anyone who wants it, e.g. synapse · read Theo's code · 23 capabilities · 0.5 s.

1. INT. BACK OFFICE — NIGHT Nine tabs0:00 – 0:13

Black. Rain on a window. A PC fan spins up; a hard drive chatters. Light comes up on the back office: one desk, a desk lamp, a 2009 monitor. We drift toward it.

On the screen, browser tabs open one after another: Back Office v2.3, Supplier portal, SMS box, workload.xls… The RAM gadget's needle climbs into the red.

Martin (o.s., from the workshop, tired)~170 wpm

Theo — where's the parts list again?

Theo~185 wpm

Tab four. No — five.

(beat)

It's the one with the spanner.

Picture

Diorama wide → slow push to the monitor. Screen: the Windows 7 desktop, nine tabs piling up, the CPU/RAM gadget dial.

Sound

Rain, PC fan, HDD clicks, a workshop radio far off. Tabs open with small real clicks. No music yet.

Why

The pain is shown by two people, not a caption.

2. INT. BACK OFFICE — NIGHT (CONT'D) The handover0:13 – 0:42

Theo (quietly)

Seventeen years.

The tabs close, one by one. A brass bell rings softly: Barnaby's window opens.

Theo

On Barnaby's screen, a floor plan draws itself, room by room. Six rooms light up.

Barnaby~155 wpm

Barnaby

Noted.

Who's got too much on this week?

Add it.

Good idea.
