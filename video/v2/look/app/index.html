Signpost: the dealt direction

World
British cycle-route and road signing (Kinneir & Calvert; the blue cycle-route signs): legends made to be read at a distance by everyone. Martin reads from a step back; this system was designed for exactly that.
Thesis
Barnaby is signed like a route: his answer is the big legend, the places he went are posted on a blue route sign, and anything that needs your say-so is a yellow temporary panel. No bubbles, no italic, no log.
Palette
route bluewaiting for youlegend blackwhite panelkept shut / a problem only
Type
Corbel throughout (installed, Windows 7 era), sentence case; Kinneir's two weights: heavier black on white, lighter white on blue. Consolas only inside the folded technical detail and the diff.
First viewport
White conversation: a pictogram column (the bell tile, the time) then the legend; the question at 30px in quotes, the answer at 42px. Right: the route sign "Where he went", this request's stops at 24px with a mark each, earlier requests folded to a line. Footer: Hold to talk, "or hold the space bar", the input.
Signature
Panels with a rounded inset border, the sign's own edge: white on the blue route, black on the yellow say-so panel.
Raised by the hand it beat
  • From the type specimen: hierarchy by scale alone; one face, no boxes round words.
  • From the bitmap specimen: every gap a whole multiple of a 6px tile.
  • From the Factory catalogue: every request numbered and timed ("No. 4 · 09:07"); the "What he can do" drawer is the index.
  • From the centre-rail setting: a full printed-mark vocabulary for state (filled square, key, ring, tick, cross, no-entry), never colour alone.
  • From the transit diagram: never shrink the network into a miniature; the current request's stops stay full size.
Honest risk
The yellow panel is loud by design; if two approvals arrive together the screen gets a lot of yellow. Corbel's figures need lining numerals switched on (done in CSS). Road signs are a public, not a family, object: it belongs to Martin's world as a cyclist more than to the shop counter.

Ticket: the challenger (competitive)

World
Dealt from the catalogue as a jet-age airline ticket wallet, fused with the shop's own numbered repair-ticket book. The form and its grammar come from the wallet; every fact comes from the product.
Thesis
Every request is a numbered, timed ticket; what he did is the carbon copy; the say-so is a coupon you tear off; every yes and no is stamped and stays. Nothing disappears, it gets stamped.
Palette
carrier navy (ink, not ground)stamp redcarbon purplecanary copywallet
Type
Franklin Gothic (Office): Book for the words, Demi for titles, Medium/Demi Condensed capitals only for a ticket's field labels (Ticket no., Time, What it reads).
Signature
The perforated tear line and the stub: the approval is a coupon attached under the ticket that asked for it; a decision becomes a slightly crooked rubber stamp.
Verdict
Competitive. It wins on audience identification (Martin writes repair tickets every day; this is the counter, not the road) and loses on product clarity at a distance (field boxes and a canary column are denser than a sign).
Honest risk
Busier: more rules, more labels; condensed capitals are small. Navy ink and red stamps sit closer to "airline" than "bike shop" unless the director wants the ticket book's literal colours. The canary column is a big warm field next to white.

Impeccable's pick, not built: the trade parts catalogue

World
The cycle trade's parts catalogues and dealer manuals: exploded views with numbered callouts, a parts list, black line and one spot colour on white.
Thesis
Barnaby's answer carries numbered callouts that point at the things he looked at; "What he can do" is the parts list of the house.
Why it isn't built
It topped my own list, and the dice dealt the third. It is also the nearest to the film's dealer-manual floor plan, so it is the familiar choice: strong, but where most runs on this brief land. Say the word and it becomes a third skin.

Honest risk: familiarity. It would read as "the film's floor plan, on the app".

Today's look, for comparison

What both skins change in how it works

  • Activity reads in plain words built from each ability's own description: "Looked at repair jobs due on Tue 24 Nov", "Gave a job to a different mechanic", "Used “Hand back a bike in one step”". The method, path, arguments and result sit folded under each line.
  • The builder's ten raw steps become six plain ones, said in the present while he is at it: Started on the missing piece, Read the notes left with the code, Read the code, Wrote the change (3 files), Ran the checks (All 22 pass), Finished writing it (53 seconds).
  • Every yes and not now stays in the conversation and on the route; today the card simply vanishes.
  • The Build card is laid out as What it does / What it reads / What it changes / Checks / If you change your mind, with the buttons before the diff; opening the change brings it into view and says "3 files, 102 lines added, nothing removed".
  • The key card adds "Each time: he still asks you before he does it" only for abilities that change something (true by the tier policy).
  • The drawer is "What he can do": every ability by area in plain words with its state mark, the technical name small beside it, the connect form below; it takes the right column's place, so nothing is half covered.

For the director to decide

  1. Signpost, Ticket, or neither (a re-roll, the pick as a third skin, or the category standard). Until then / stays exactly as it was.
  2. The film. The script and the Lisbon PC screens show today's look; switching changes what the "real app" beat shows.
  3. Wording on the household screens: "What he can do" for "Capabilities", "Where he went" / "Work done" for the trace, the status as a sentence. The connect form keeps the app's real wording.
  4. DESIGN.md still describes Livery; it gets rewritten from the chosen skin once you pick, not before.

How the roll was made

Mode: operate (a working screen). Seven candidates from the audience's world, none from the tools they operate, ordered by resonance before the dice:

  1. The cycle trade's parts catalogue and dealer manual (exploded views, numbered callouts).
  2. The repair tag and job ticket on every bike (manila card, rubber stamps, tear-off stub).
  3. British cycle-route and road signing (Kinneir & Calvert; blue route signs; legible at distance). Dealt.
  4. The workshop whiteboard job board (columns per mechanic, magnets, read from across the room).
  5. The servants' bell board (the literal butler: one bell per room, a flag drops).
  6. The Ordnance Survey map and its legend (club rides; the key to the symbols).
  7. The carbon triplicate order pad and the counter day book.
