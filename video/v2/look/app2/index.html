Barnaby's display, as a real app

The director's verdict on the last round: the default has the AI tell, and the road-sign and ticket skins are gimmicks, "and not like real apps". This round plays the category standard straight, at the craft level of Linear, Things 3, Messages and Mail on macOS, and the Claude desktop app. Barnaby lives in the details: the bell mark, one brass accent, his voice in the copy, approvals that read like a careful colleague asking, and a voice control that feels native. An answer, at the owner's 1440×900

In product terms

Written before any pixels: PRODUCT-NOTES.md. In short:

  • Who and where. The owner, 61, talking from a step back to a 2009-era PC at about 1440×900 (a pixel there is as big as on a modern 24-inch 1080p screen, so type is not scaled down for it); staff on the same machine; a developer at 1920×1080; a compact window about 560 wide.
  • Jobs, in order. Talk (voice first, typing equal); read the answer; know what he is doing without noise; say yes or no with full understanding; see what he can do; connect a system.
  • States. Empty, ready, working, waiting for your say-so, listening, error: each has one calm, legible form.

Design decisions, and why

DecisionWhy
Light, white canvas; a neutral inspector; one soft shadow, only on the card that needs youDaylight, strip lights and a glossy panel; a 61-year-old reader. The card is the one raised thing because it is the one thing asking.
Segoe UI throughout (Variable on Windows 11), one fixed scale; mono only for codeThe platform's own face reads as a real Windows app and is on every machine it runs on. No font download, no costume face.
Toolbar · conversation · inspector · composerThe conventions of Mail, Linear and the Claude app: you know where everything is before you notice the look.
Author lines, no bubbles; current answer 22px, history 18pxBubbles and italic "bot voice" were the AI tell. A thread with names and times reads like Mail; the size step is how the owner finds the answer from a step back.
Status as one sentence in the present tense ("Looking at repair jobs due on Tue 24 Nov", "Waiting for your say-so")Know what he is doing without noise. Only the line in the conversation moves; the toolbar and the step hold still.
"What he did" in plain words, a disclosure on each step for the technical record; earlier requests fold to short lines; a yes is listed before the act it allowed"Show where he has been" without a developer trace on the owner's screen.
The decision card: icon, plain title, the why or what-it-does as a lead line, a facts table, "See the change" (with its size) and "Technical detail" folded, then a footer with how it may be answered and Not now / YesReads top to bottom the way a decision is made. The footer docks to the bottom edge while the change is open, so yes and no are never scrolled away. Code and keys need a click; ordinary changes accept a spoken yes, and the card says which.
Every yes and no stays in the thread, naming the thing ("You said yes: Ten pairs of the Shimano resin pads from Madison…")Consent is visible afterwards, in his words, not the system's arguments.
Hold to talk is the composer's primary control, with "or hold the space bar"; held, it turns brass, shows the level and says "Listening…"; "Let go when you've finished."Voice is the main input; it should feel like dictation on a phone, not a feature.
"What he can do" takes the inspector's place: an inset grouped list by area, a state word on every ability, technical names behind one switchMartin reads states; the developer turns the names on.
Brass (#AA7C27) only for his mark, the waiting state, listening, focus and selectionIdentity in precise details, not a costume; the colour means "Barnaby" or "your turn".
